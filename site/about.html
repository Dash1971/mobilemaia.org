Privacy & credits.

A small, simple website.

The homepage and research articles add no analytics, advertising, tracking pixels, third-party fonts, or cookies. They do not use browser storage or require an account. Their images, styles, and JavaScript are served from the same site.

The site is hosted by GitHub Pages. GitHub states that it logs and stores visitors’ IP addresses for security, whether or not visitors have signed into GitHub. See GitHub Pages’ data-collection explanation and the GitHub Privacy Statement. External links lead to services with their own policies.

The Android app runs play and analysis locally. It has no accounts, ads, or tracking. Downloading the app and voluntarily sharing a PGN or diagnostics involve the services you choose. See the app’s privacy and licensing documentation.

Experimental browser inference test.

The inference benchmark downloads your selected Maia model (5M by default, or 79M for comparison) from GitHub and a pinned WebAssembly runtime from jsDelivr only when you start a test. Those providers receive normal download requests. Inference and results stay on your device; the prototype adds no analytics or automatic result uploads. You can optionally save these downloads in a cache reserved for the experiment and clear them with its Clear saved downloads button. Exporting a report saves a local file for you to share manually. The runtime JavaScript and its licence notices are served from this site.

Built on shared work.

Mobile Maia is independently developed. It is not an official application of, or endorsed by, Maia Chess, the University of Toronto CSSLab, Stockfish, Lichess, En Croissant, or Chessnut.

  • Maia-3 — the human-like chess model developed by the University of Toronto’s Computational Social Science Lab.
  • Stockfish and multistockfish — local engine analysis.
  • Lichess Flutter Chessground, the Cburnett chess pieces by Colin M. L. Burnett, and the Lichess opening database — board, pieces, and opening names.
  • En Croissant — the source of adapted game-review classification heuristics in the Android app, and inspiration for a welcoming project homepage.

Full component licences and corresponding source references are recorded in the app’s third-party notices. The opt-in inference lab uses ONNX Runtime Web, with its MIT licence and third-party notices.
